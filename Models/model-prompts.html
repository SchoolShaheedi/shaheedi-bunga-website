Parikrama Model Set

Thirteen prompts for Claude Design — eight buildings and five pieces of ground. Generate, download the GLB, and file under the name given; the build script matches on those names.

Start with one

Make harmandir first and nothing else. I will open it, check the scale and orientation actually came out as asked, and drop it into the page — then we both look at it before you spend an evening on the other twelve.

Paste this first, every time

The block that goes in every prompt

This is the half that does the real work. It is what makes eight separately generated models sit together in one scene at the same scale, facing the same way, in the same material language. Paste it above each prompt below.

Shared spec — prepend to all eight
Model this as a single 3D object for a website. It is one of a set of eight
that all sit together in the same scene, so keep it consistent with that.

Scale and orientation — these matter, the models are dropped into a shared
scene and are not rescaled by hand:
  · Real-world scale, in metres.
  · +Y is up. The object rests on the ground plane at y = 0.
  · Origin centred on the footprint.
  · The front elevation faces +Z.

Style:
  · Low to mid poly. Clean flat surfaces and a readable silhouette matter
    more than fine detail.
  · No image textures. Flat coloured materials only.
  · Muted, chalky palette: lime-plaster off-white, warm sandstone, weathered
    brass-gold. Nothing shiny — no chrome, no glass, no mirror finishes.
  · Nineteenth-century Panjabi architecture. Weathered and hand-built, not
    new and not CGI-perfect.

Exclude: ground plane, base plate, water, terrain, surrounding buildings,
trees, people, text, signage, and any lighting rig. The object only.
Eight parts

The buildings

Four are marked core — the page cannot be rebuilt without them. The other four make the scene better but I can fall back to procedural geometry for any of them, so do those only if you have the appetite.

harmandir

Harmandir Sahib

Core

One, standing in the water at the centre. The most looked-at object on the page.

Prompt
Harmandir Sahib, the shrine at Amritsar, as a standalone building.

A LOW SQUARE building — 12.25 m on each side and only about 15 m to the tip
of its finial, so it is noticeably wider than it is tall. Three storeys.

Ground storey, about 5 m: a white marble base band roughly 1.5 m high, and
everything above that band is gilded. Seven narrow arched openings to a side.
Above it a deep overhanging eave carried on carved brackets, running right
round the building.

Second storey, about 3.5 m, set back slightly: gilded, its surfaces covered
in small embossed panels with narrow windows. A second, shallower eave above.

Then a flat terrace with a low parapet of small ROUNDED pinnacles. Four
compact domed pavilions on short columns at the corners, and two smaller ones
spaced along each side between them.

In the middle of the terrace, a small square chamber. On top of that, a
fluted dome shaped like an inverted lotus — only about a third of the
building's width — sitting in a flared ring of lotus petals on a low drum,
topped by a slender kalash finial.

No minarets, no towers, no spires, no onion dome. This is not a mosque.
Do not include the marble platform it stands on, the causeway, or the water.
bunga-tall

Bunga — three storeys

Core

Used roughly eight times around the ring, varied by scale and rotation.

Prompt
A bunga — one of the tall narrow townhouses that stood shoulder to shoulder
in a ring around the sacred pool at Amritsar in the nineteenth century,
before they were demolished. This is the tall type.

About 17 m wide, 12 m deep, 13 m tall. Three storeys, each set back slightly
from the one below so the building steps inward as it rises.

Every storey is an open arcaded verandah: a row of six or seven cusped arches
carried on square piers, open right through, with a dark recessed room behind
them. A moulded string course marks the division between storeys.

A flat roof terrace with a low parapet of small rounded pinnacles, and a small
domed pavilion on four slender columns at each corner.

Lime-plastered brick, weathered, warm off-white with patches of ochre. This is
a working residence and school, not a palace — keep it modest.

No dome on the main roof. No gold anywhere.
bunga-mid

Bunga — two storeys

Core

Used roughly six times. Needs to read as a different building from the tall one, not a shrunk copy.

Prompt
A bunga at Amritsar — one of the ring of nineteenth-century townhouses around
the sacred pool. This is the squatter, wider type, and it should read as a
clearly different building from the tall three-storey one, not a shrunk copy.

About 14 m wide, 11 m deep, 9.5 m tall. Two storeys only, the upper set back
slightly.

The ground storey is an open arcaded verandah of five broad cusped arches on
square piers, with a dark recessed room behind. The upper storey is more
closed: a solid plastered wall with four small arched windows and one
projecting carved wooden balcony (jharokha) in the middle.

Flat roof, low parapet of small rounded pinnacles, and a small domed pavilion
on slender columns at two of the four corners only — the other two left plain.

Weathered lime plaster in warm off-white with patches of dusty pink and ochre.
No dome on the main roof. No gold.
shaheedan

Shaheedan da Bunga

Core

One. The building the charity is named for — the hero of the whole page.

Prompt
Shaheedan da Bunga — the bunga of the shaheedi jatha at Amritsar. This is the
hero building of the set, so give it more care and detail than the others.

About 16 m wide, 12 m deep, 15 m to the tip of the finial. Three storeys of
open arcaded verandahs, each set back slightly from the one below, six cusped
arches to a storey on square piers, dark recessed rooms behind.

A flat roof terrace with a low parapet of small rounded pinnacles. A compact
gilded domed pavilion on slender columns at each of the four corners. In the
centre of the terrace, a single gilded dome shaped like an inverted lotus,
sitting on a low drum, topped by a kalash finial.

Warm off-white weathered lime plaster, with weathered gold on the domes and
finials only — nowhere else.

Grander than an ordinary bunga, but still recognisably a working building
rather than a palace.
bunga-low

Bunga — single storey

Optional

Used three or four times. Modelled on the one surviving photograph of Shaheedan da Bunga, which shows a much humbler building than the version above.

Prompt
A single low bunga at the water's edge at Amritsar, of the plainest kind.

One storey only. About 19 m wide, 11 m deep, and 7 m to the top of the
parapet, standing on a low terrace one step above the ground.

A row of six cusped-arch openings along the front, with a decorated frieze
band running in a continuous strip above them.

A flat roof with a low parapet of small rounded pinnacles, and one small domed
pavilion on slender columns rising above one END of the roof — off-centre, not
in the middle.

Plain, weathered, humble. Off-white lime plaster going grey in places. No
gold, no dome on the main roof.
ramgarhia

Ramgarhia Bunga

Optional

One. Its two towers are the tallest things on the skyline and the one bunga still standing today.

Prompt
The Ramgarhia Bunga at Amritsar — a block with two tall watchtowers. One of
the very few bungas that still stands.

The block: rectangular, about 22 m wide, 13 m deep, 12 m tall, in weathered
red-brown brick banded with lime plaster, arcaded along the front.

Rising from it, TWO SLENDER OCTAGONAL TOWERS about 48 m tall, standing about
12 m apart. Each tower tapers as it rises and is divided into seven stages by
projecting moulded rings, alternating red brick and pale plaster between the
rings. Near the top each widens into a balcony ring, and is capped by a small
open pavilion on slender columns carrying a ribbed white dome.

The towers are VERY tall and VERY thin — that extreme proportion is the whole
character of this building, so do not thicken them.
deori

Darshani Deori

Optional

One, at the land end of the causeway. May already be inside your existing darbar-sahib export.

Prompt
The Darshani Deori — the gateway building at the land end of the causeway at
Amritsar, the arch every pilgrim walks through to reach the shrine.

About 19 m wide, 13 m deep, 16 m to the finial. Two storeys.

The lower storey is largely solid, with ONE great cusped archway cut right
through the middle of it, tall enough to walk through, framed by a gilded
surround. The upper storey is set back and open — an arcaded gallery of small
cusped arches.

Flat roof, low parapet of rounded pinnacles, one gilded dome in the centre,
and a small white domed pavilion on slender columns at each of the four
corners.

White and off-white lime plaster throughout. Gold ONLY on the arch surround
and the central dome.
akal-takht

Akal Takht

Optional

One, on the entrance bank. Without it the skyline does not read as Amritsar.

Prompt
The Akal Takht at Amritsar, as a standalone building.

About 25 m wide, 16 m deep, 22 m tall. Five storeys, each stepping inward from
the one below so the building tapers as it rises.

Every storey has an arcaded front of cusped arches, with flat roofs and low
parapets of small rounded pinnacles at each setback.

Crowned by one large gilded dome with a kalash finial, and a small gilded
domed pavilion on slender columns at each corner of the top roof.

White lime plaster below. Gold ONLY on the domes and finials.
Five more

The ground

The walkway, the water, the bridge and the two things that repeat all over the scene. Same shared block on top of each.

parikrama

The walkway

One. The ground the whole journey is walked along.

Prompt
The parikrama — the marble walkway that rings the sacred pool at Amritsar —
as a standalone piece of ground.

A SQUARE RING, 186 m across on the outside and 156 m across on the inside, so
the walkway itself is 15 m wide all the way round. Its surface sits about
1.9 m above the water.

Flat marble paving in warm off-white, laid in large slabs, with a repeating
inlaid geometric border of darker stone running along both edges of the walk.
A low balustrade of small square posts about 0.6 m high along the INNER edge
only — the water side. Broad shallow steps down into the water at four points,
one in the middle of each side.

No water, no buildings, no lamps, no trees.
sarovar

The tank

One. I will most likely keep the top surface live in code so it can ripple and reflect, and use your model for the basin and edges.

Prompt
The sarovar — the basin of the sacred pool at Amritsar — as a standalone
piece of ground.

A square tank 156 m across. Stone sides dropping about 4 m, with one broad
step running all the way round the inside near the top, just below the
waterline.

The water surface itself is a single flat plane filling the tank, almost
still, with only a very slight wide undulation across it. Deep blue-green,
nearly black.

Keep the water surface as its own separate object or material so it can be
swapped — do not merge it into the stone.

No walkway, no buildings, no bridge.
causeway

The pul

One. Runs from the gateway out to the shrine.

Prompt
The causeway at Amritsar — the pul that crosses the water to the shrine — as
a standalone object.

A straight marble bridge 62 m long and 6.4 m wide, its deck standing about
1.8 m above the water. Flat marble paving along the deck. A low balustrade of
small turned posts carrying a moulded rail along BOTH sides. At the far end,
a small square landing slightly wider than the bridge itself.

Long, narrow and low — it should read as a walkway across water, not a road
bridge.

No water, no buildings, no lamps.
lamp

Lamp standard

Repeated around fifty times, so its silhouette does a lot of work.

Prompt
A single ornamental lamp standard from the walkway at Amritsar.

About 4.2 m tall and slender. A moulded stone base about 0.4 m high, a fluted
brass shaft rising from it, a small moulded collar near the top, and a single
round glass globe about 0.3 m across on top.

Weathered brass going green in the hollows. The globe is plain frosted glass,
unlit.

One lamp only, standing upright. No ground, no light source, no glow.
nishan-sahib

Nishan Sahib

Two or three, beside the bungas.

Prompt
A Nishan Sahib — the Sikh flagpole that stands beside a gurdwara.

A tall straight pole about 16 m high and slender, wrapped along its whole
length in cloth, with a small pointed finial at the very top.

A triangular saffron flag flying from near the top, roughly 3 m along its top
edge, tapering to a point, hanging as if in light wind rather than stretched
flat.

The pole and the flag only. No building, no base platform, no ground.

Also drop in the one you already made

The existing darbar-sahib export, under whatever name it downloads as. It has the platform, water, causeway and gateway baked into one object, so I may use parts of it or none of it — but I want to open it and see what is actually in there.

GLB, not OBJ, if you get the choice

GLB carries the geometry and the materials in one file. OBJ splits them, and the .mtl gets separated from the .obj embarrassingly easily. If you end up with OBJ, keep both files together and give them the same basename.

Then

What I do with them

  1. Open the Harmandir first, on its own. Poly count, whether the materials survived, whether the scale and orientation actually came out as asked. Generated models routinely ignore an origin instruction, and it is far quicker to find that out on one model than on thirteen.
  2. Then the rest. Poly count, whether the materials survived, whether the scale and orientation actually came out as asked. Generated models routinely ignore an origin instruction, and it is quicker for me to correct that once than to fight it later.
  3. Convert them at build time. The Three.js build the site uses has no GLTF loader in it, so rather than bundle one I will parse each file once in Node and emit the geometry as plain arrays. That keeps the page a single self-contained file and lets me relight everything from scratch.
  4. Rebuild Draft 1 around them. Same six chapters, same camera path, same amrit vela — but every building replaced by a real model instead of the procedural approximation that has been the weak point of the whole page.
