What is actually happening

Phase 1 · 0–35%

The chain

The tuk sits as one unbroken run of letters — no spaces at all. This is larivaar, the form Gurbani is written in. A reader who has not been taught cannot tell where one word ends and the next begins.

Phase 2 · 35–62%

The break

Gaps open between the words, left to right, each one a fraction behind the last. It reads as a chain unlinking rather than a block stretching — which is the whole point, because the divisions are being found, not applied.

Phase 3 · 62–72%

Pad ched

The line settles into pad ched — word-separated. Eight units where a moment ago there was one. The English gloss fades in beneath.

Phase 4 · 72–100%

Recitation

Each akhara lights in turn at reading pace, the way an ustad would take a student through it. Not a typing effect — a reading effect, and it settles rather than races.

Why this belongs on this page and nowhere else. Every other class page can be described. Santhiya can be performed — the scroll does the exact thing the class teaches, which is finding the divisions in the chain. Nobody visiting will have seen a website do this.

Two things I need from you. First, the English gloss above is my working translation and should be checked by an ustad before it goes near the live site. Second — if there is a teaching example where breaking the chain in the wrong place changes the meaning, that would be the single strongest thing on the whole page. I have not invented one, and I would not: it needs to come from the vidyala.
