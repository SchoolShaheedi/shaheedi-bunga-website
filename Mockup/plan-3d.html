Shaheedi Bunga · proposal, not yet built

The Bunga in three dimensions

A plan for a genuinely 3D website — what it would be, how it would be built, what it costs, and where it could fail. Nothing has been created yet. This is for you to approve, change or reject first.

One

What the best 3D sites actually do

I looked at what is winning now. Three findings matter for us.

  • One hard idea, executed cleanly. The sites that stand out pick a single concept — a drivable physics world, one object rendered with real weight — rather than stacking effects. The ones that stack effects look dated within a year.
  • Scroll is the storytelling engine. Cartier, Shopify and others drive the whole experience from scroll position, sequencing 3D scenes rather than moving a 2D page. This is exactly the architecture we already built for the journey — so that work is not wasted.
  • 3D as framing, not spectacle. The most admired work uses WebGL for atmosphere and composition, and lets the content stay the subject.
The trap. Most "3D websites" are a rotating object nobody needs, bolted to a normal page. If we do this, the 3D has to be the site's argument, not decoration on top of it.
Two

The technical constraint, and how it is solved

Everything I deliver runs inside a sandboxed page with a strict content policy: no external scripts. That is why I could not use GSAP or Lenis earlier. It applies to Three.js too.

But I tested it and there is a clean way through. I can download the Three.js library to your machine and inline it into the page at build time. I verified this today:

BuildSizeGzippedVerdict
three r170 module692 KB172 KBUsable — fetched successfully
three r160 minified670 KB167 KBUsable fallback

For context, the photographic journey already weighs about 5 MB. Adding 692 KB of library is a 14% increase — negligible. Real WebGL is available to us. Three.js is MIT-licensed, so inlining is entirely legitimate.

What is not available: ready-made 3D models. You have no .glb files and none exist for a bunga. Everything would be built procedurally in code — geometry generated from arches, domes, pillars and lathes. That means a warm, stylised, low-poly building, not a photoreal one. I think stylised is the right answer anyway, but you should picture that, not a video-game render.
Two b

motionsites.ai — what it is, and what to take from it

I went through it. It is worth being precise about what it actually sells, because it changes how we should use it.

It is a library of AI prompts, not a library of websites. You copy a prompt and paste it into Lovable, Bolt, Cursor or Claude, and it generates a landing page. The categories are 3d Website, threejs, Animated Backgrounds, Hero, Carousel, and industry sets — SaaS, Fintech, Agency, Wellness. Named templates include 3D Future Flora (explicitly Three.js), Cyberpunk Reveal, Obsidian, TerraVision and Nature Ritual.

Worth taking

  • Animated backgrounds as atmosphere. A living, slow-moving ground behind content — this is the single most transferable idea and it suits a dusk-lit bunga exactly.
  • The reveal hero. Their "Cyberpunk Reveal" pattern — content uncovering itself on load — is the same mechanism as the Gurmukhi inking we already built. Confirms that instinct was right.
  • 3D Future Flora's particle system. Growth rendered as particles. Repointed at our subject that becomes something real: particles settling into a Gurmukhi letterform, or lamps lighting one by one across the globe as each student joins.

Worth avoiding

  • Using a template as the design. These are built for SaaS and agency landing pages. Dropping a Sikh school into "Aurex Finance" would produce a site that looks like everyone else's, and the research in section one says stacked-effect template sites date within about a year.
  • Effects for their own sake. Their showcase optimises for "impress and amaze". Your visitors are parents looking for Tuesday's class time and grandparents reading Gurbani.
One practical note: motionsites.ai now offers an MCP. If you connect it to this account, I could pull their prompt content directly rather than working from screenshots, and lift specific techniques with precision. Your call — say the word and I will tell you exactly where to connect it.
Three

Three concepts

Each is a different answer to "why does this site need 3D at all?"

A · The Bunga Recommended

You are named after a building. A ਬੁੰਗਾ was a residence and school beside a gurdwara where students lived while they learned. So: build the bunga. The visitor arrives outside it at dusk, and scroll carries them through the door and inside. Each room is a class. The santhiya room has pothis open on stands. The kirtan room holds a dilruba and taus. The jori room. The akhaara is the courtyard. The library is upstairs, and its window looks out over the world.

It is not decoration — it is the organisation's own metaphor, made literal. And it resolves the thing you have been asking for since the beginning: a journey into each class, where "into" finally means something spatial.

Risk: the most build effort. Architecture generated in code takes real time to get right, and a badly-proportioned bunga would look worse than a photograph.

B · The Network Strong second

A slowly turning globe. Leicester at the centre, with arcs out to Panjab (ten students living and learning together), Scotland, Germany, and scattered points for the online students. Scroll pulls you into each node and that place's story opens.

This tells the single most remarkable fact about you — that you are a school which exports teachers — and a globe is genuinely the right form for it. It is also far less work than A.

Risk: globes are a familiar trope. It would need real restraint to avoid looking like a corporate "global presence" slide.

C · The Shastar Table Detail piece

Shastar and instruments as objects you can pick up and turn — tulwar, dhal, soti, dilruba, taus, jori — on a dark ground, lit like a museum case. Each one names itself in Gurmukhi as you rotate it.

Risk: not a whole site. Better as one section inside something larger.

My recommendation: A as the building, B as the view from its library window, C as the akhaara wall. One world, three moments. The bunga carries the site; the globe carries the international story; the shastar carry the detail.

Concept B lives here · arcs drawn as curves · gentle auto-rotation

08

The door out

Back outside. Timetable, register, donate. The practical pages are ordinary, fast HTML — never 3D.

Plain DOM · no WebGL dependency

Five

Performance — the real risk

This is where I have to be blunt with you. Your audience includes parents checking class times on cheap Android phones and grandparents reading Gurbani. WebGL is the heaviest thing the web can ask of a device. When you last chose, you said full effects everywhere — I built that, and the scroll motion could not even be verified in my own tooling.

What I propose instead is not a compromise on ambition. It is three doors into the same building.

DeviceWhat they get
Desktop and modern phonesFull 3D bunga, real-time lighting, the globe, interactive shastar
Older or low-memory devicesThe photographic journey we already built — the same chapters, the same words, no WebGL
Reduced-motion, or WebGL unavailableA clean, fast, fully readable page. Nothing breaks.

Detection would be a real capability check — WebGL2 support, device memory, and a short benchmark on the first frames — not guesswork from the user agent.

Non-negotiable: the timetable, registration and donate pages never depend on WebGL. A parent must be able to find Tuesday's class time on a five-year-old phone in under three seconds. If the 3D ever threatens that, the 3D loses.
Six

Build phases

Each phase ends with something you can look at and judge. Nothing is a black box until the end.

Phase 0

Proof of concept

One scene only: the bunga exterior at dusk, scroll-driven orbit, inlined Three.js. Confirms the whole approach works in the sandbox and that it looks good. Small and disposable.

Phase 1

The building

Full procedural geometry — domes, arches, courtyard, upper floor. Lighting and materials. No content yet. This is the phase where proportions get judged.

Phase 2

The camera path

Scroll drives the walkthrough, room to room, with the content panels appearing alongside. Real text from the audit — the real timetable, real class descriptions.

Phase 3

The globe and the shastar

Concept B in the library window; the rotatable objects in the courtyard.

Phase 4

Tiering and fallbacks

Capability detection, the photographic fallback path, reduced-motion, and testing on a genuinely slow device profile.

Phase 5

The practical site

Timetable, classes, library, register, donate, about — plain and fast, wired to the real data from the audit. This is the part that actually serves parents.
