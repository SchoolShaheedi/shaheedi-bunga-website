Amritsar Image Brief

Prompts to generate, photographs to license, and the checks that decide whether a generated image is usable. Written for the parikrama homepage, but the rules hold for anything showing the Darbar Sahib.

Read this before generating anything

Do not generate Harmandir Sahib if you can photograph it instead. Image models consistently get it wrong — they add minarets, swap the fluted inverted-lotus dome for a bulbous onion dome, put white marble where the gold should be, and invent Gurmukhi that reads as nonsense. A distorted Darbar Sahib on a Sikh charity’s homepage is a bigger problem than a plain one. There are thousands of freely licensed photographs, four of which are already in your Mockup/refs folder.

Generate what cannot be photographed instead. The seventy bungas were demolished. Nobody can photograph the parikrama as it stood in 1840, or a santhiya class inside Shaheedan da Bunga. That is where generated images earn their place, and where prompts 1–4 below are aimed.

Step one

The house style block

Paste this ahead of every prompt below. It is what keeps nine separate images looking like one set rather than nine stock photos, and it is the half of the prompt you should tune first if the results feel wrong.

Prepend to every prompt
Photographic realism, not illustration or 3D render. Full-frame camera, 35mm
lens, f/4, natural and available light only. Restrained desaturated palette:
deep blue-black night, warm leaf-gold, pale warm marble, dusty ochre. Deep
shadow, no fill light, no HDR, no bloom, no lens flare, no colour grading
toward teal or orange. Calm and documentary, never dramatic. Nothing centred
and symmetrical unless stated. Aspect ratio 3:2 horizontal.

If your tool takes parameters Midjourney: append --ar 3:2 --style raw --s 120. Anything that offers a “photographic” or “natural” preset, use it. Turn off any “enhance”, “vivid” or “cinematic” toggle — all three fight the house style.

Prompts 1–4

Generate these: the world that is gone

No photograph of any of this exists. These four carry the story the homepage is telling, and they are the ones worth spending generations on.

01

The ring of bungas, c. 1840

Chapter 2 — “Around this water stood more than seventy bungas.” The single most important image in the set.

Prompt
A wide view across a large square sacred water tank in Amritsar in the 1840s,
before the surrounding buildings were cleared. Along every bank stands an
unbroken wall of tall narrow Punjabi townhouses, three and four storeys high,
built shoulder to shoulder in lime-plastered brick and sandstone. Each has
deep arcaded verandahs with cusped arches, carved wooden balconies and
jharokhas, fretwork screens, flat roof terraces, and small domed rooftop
pavilions on slender columns. Two tall slender octagonal watchtowers with
ribbed white domes rise above the roofline on one side. A broad marble
walkway runs between the buildings and the water. In the middle of the tank,
reached by one narrow marble causeway, sits a low square gilded shrine, small
in the frame. Early morning, flat pale light, thin mist on the water, glassy
reflections. Muted ochre, lime white, dusty rose, weathered teak, pale gold.
Wide-angle architectural documentary photograph.
Negative: minarets, crescent moons, Taj Mahal, Mughal tomb, mosque, Hindu
temple shikhara, gopuram, modern buildings, cars, electric lights, cables,
crowds, tourists, text, signage, watermark, lens flare, tilt-shift, HDR

Reject if the surrounding buildings look like a single palace rather than many separate houses jammed together — the whole point is that seventy different jathas each built their own. Variety of height, width and colour is the test.

02

A class inside a bunga

Chapter 3 — “Santhiya. Shastar vidya. Itihaas. Kirtan.” Also usable on the classes and about pages.

Prompt
Interior of an arcaded upper room in a nineteenth-century Punjabi building.
Thick lime-plastered walls, a row of cusped arches open to a verandah on one
side, worn stone floor, a low wooden takht with a cloth canopy. Eight or nine
Sikh men and boys in plain cotton kurtas and dastaars sit cross-legged on
rugs in a loose semicircle facing an older teacher. Handwritten pothis on
wooden rehals in front of them. A single oil lamp and the cold blue light of
early morning coming through the arches, lighting faces from the side.
Everything else in shadow. Quiet, ordinary, mid-lesson. Warm brown, indigo,
saffron, lime white. Available-light documentary photograph, shot from the
back of the room at seated height.
Negative: text on the pages, legible script, modern clothing, electric light,
posed group portrait, everyone looking at camera, extra fingers, distorted
hands, distorted faces, weapons pointed at people, incense, gold ornament

Reject if any hand, face or dastaar is malformed — this image shows real people, so anatomy failures are disqualifying, not fixable. Also reject if the pothis show legible lettering; generated Gurmukhi is always nonsense. Crop it out or regenerate.

03

Shaheedan da Bunga at night

Chapter 5 — “The one we are named for.”

Prompt
A single tall narrow four-storey Punjabi townhouse at the edge of a marble
walkway beside still black water, photographed at night from across the
walkway. Lime-plastered brick, three tiers of deep arcaded verandahs with
cusped arches stacked one above the other, carved wooden balconies, a flat
roof terrace with a low parapet and four small domed pavilions at the
corners. Warm oil lamplight glows out of the arches on the middle floor only;
every other opening is dark. A dark blue pre-dawn sky behind. Two or three
oil lamps on tall posts stand on the walkway. Long exposure, still water,
soft reflection of the lit windows. Muted: near-black, deep indigo, warm
amber, pale plaster. Available-light architectural photograph, no people.
Negative: minarets, mosque, temple, gold, marble palace, modern lighting,
floodlights, string lights, neon, people, cars, text, signage, lens flare

Reject if it reads as grand or palatial. A bunga was a working residence and school, not a monument. Plain, tall, a bit austere.

04

The parikrama after the clearance

Chapter 4 — “Almost all of them were pulled down.”

Prompt
A very wide empty white marble walkway curving around the edge of a large
square water tank, photographed at first light with nobody on it. On the
outer side, a low uniform modern white arcade replaces what used to stand
there. The walkway is far too wide for the buildings around it. A line of
plain lamp posts recedes into the distance. Flat grey-blue dawn light, no
sun, long empty perspective, wet marble holding a faint reflection. Almost
monochrome: cool white, pale grey, one thin band of warm light on the
horizon. Architectural documentary photograph, low camera, wide angle.
Negative: crowds, people, pilgrims, gold, warm sunlight, blue sky, birds,
decorative borders, text, signage, HDR, vivid colour

Reject if it looks serene rather than emptied. The emotion is absence — too much space, too few buildings.

Prompts 5–7

Darbar Sahib, if you generate it anyway

These are written to fight the specific ways models get Harmandir Sahib wrong. The architecture is spelled out in unusual detail on purpose — that description is the part doing the work. Expect to discard most attempts, and check every survivor against the list at the end of this page.

05

Darshan across the water at amrit vela

Chapter 1 opening, or a full-bleed hero if the site goes photographic.

Prompt
Harmandir Sahib in Amritsar photographed from the far bank across still black
water, two hours before dawn. The shrine is a LOW SQUARE building, wider than
it is tall, only three storeys, standing on a white marble platform barely
above the water. Its entire exterior is embossed gold-plated copper, from a
low white marble base upward — the gold covers the whole building, not just
the top. A deep overhanging eave carried on carved brackets runs round
between the lower and upper storeys. On the flat roof: one small fluted dome
shaped like an inverted lotus, gold, sitting on a low drum, no more than a
third of the building's width, topped by a slender kalash finial. Four small
gold domed pavilions on slender columns stand at the roof corners, with
smaller domed kiosks along each parapet edge between them. A narrow white
marble causeway runs from the near bank to the shrine, lined with lamp posts.
The gold is the only lit thing in the frame; everything else is near-black.
Perfect still reflection broken into soft vertical streaks. Long exposure.
Negative: minarets, towers, spires, crescent moon, onion dome, bulbous dome,
tall building, mosque, Taj Mahal, church, white marble walls, floodlights,
blue hour teal, purple sky, crowds, boats, birds, text, signage, watermark

Reject if the building is taller than it is wide, if the dome is a fat onion rather than a shallow ribbed lotus, or if any tower rises beside it. Those three failures account for almost every bad result.

06

The gold facade, close

A texture plate — section dividers, card backgrounds, the library page.

Prompt
Extreme close detail of an embossed gold-plated copper wall panel on a Sikh
shrine. Dense repoussé floral and vine ornament hammered into the sheet,
bordered by narrow raised mouldings, with a small recessed rectangular window
opening. Centuries of handling have worn the high points to a paler warm
brass while the recesses stay dark. Raking light from one side at a shallow
angle picks out every ridge. Filling the frame, shot straight on, shallow
depth of field falling off at the edges. Warm gold, brown-black shadow, no
other colour.
Negative: faces, figures, animals, calligraphy, script, lettering, symbols,
khanda, ik onkar, arabic, sanskrit, jewels, gemstones, glitter, shine, bloom

Reject if it contains anything resembling script or a religious symbol. Generated versions of either are always wrong, and wrong here is worse than plain.

07

The causeway lamps

A quieter horizontal image for between chapters, or the contact and timetable pages.

Prompt
A narrow white marble causeway crossing still dark water at night, seen along
its length from one end at waist height. A low balustrade runs down both
sides. Ornamental lamp standards spaced evenly along both edges, each with a
single warm globe, receding into the distance and reflecting in the water as
long soft vertical streaks. The far end is lost in darkness. Nobody on the
causeway. Near-black water, cool white marble, warm amber lamplight, nothing
else. Long exposure, low camera, strong one-point perspective.
Negative: people, crowds, buildings, gold, dome, boats, bright sky, moon,
stars, coloured lights, string lights, text, signage, HDR, vignette

Reject if the perspective wobbles or the lamp spacing is irregular — the whole image is the rhythm of the lamps, so an inconsistent line kills it.

Before you use it

The reject checklist

Run every generated image past these. Any one of them is a reject, not a retouch — it is faster to generate again than to fix a wrong building.

  • Reject

    Minarets, towers or spires. Harmandir Sahib has none. If the model has given it any, it has built a mosque.

  • Reject

    A bulbous onion dome. The real dome is a shallow fluted inverted lotus sitting on a low drum, and it is small — roughly a third of the building’s width.

  • Reject

    A white marble building with a gold roof. The whole exterior is gold above a low marble base. This is the mistake I made in the 3D model before checking the photographs.

  • Reject

    Too tall. It is about 12 m square and only around 15 m to the tip of the finial — wider than it is tall. Anything tower-like is wrong.

  • Reject

    Any visible lettering. Generated Gurmukhi, Devanagari and Arabic are all nonsense, and nonsense script on a shrine is not a small error.

  • Reject

    Malformed hands, faces or dastaars. Applies to any image with people in it, without exception.

  • Reject

    A crescent moon, a cross, or an invented symbol. Models reach for these whenever they are unsure what tradition they are drawing.

  • Check

    Four corner pavilions on the roof, kiosks along the parapets. Their absence is not fatal, but their presence is a good sign the model actually knows the building.

Still needed

What I need from you

The homepage now names Scotland, Germany, the Netherlands, East London, Southall, Australia, Kuwait and Africa. That line is currently the vaguest sentence on the page, and it is describing the thing that makes the charity unusual.

  1. Which country in Africa, and which city? “Africa” on its own reads as filler. One named town does more work than a continent.

  2. City or gurdwara for each of the others. Netherlands, Australia, Kuwait, Scotland, Germany — and which gurdwaras in East London and Southall.

  3. The Panjab vidyala. Town, roughly when it opened, who teaches there, and whether there are photographs.

  4. What each of these actually is. A former student teaching on their own account is a different claim from a Shaheedi Bunga branch, and a UK charity has to be careful which one it says in public.

  5. Roughly how many students in each place. Even “about fifteen” is worth more than nothing, and numbers are what turn a list of countries into evidence.
